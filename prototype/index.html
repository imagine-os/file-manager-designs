Beyond the cube

Facet put one set of files on a cube, one axis per face. The last step of its tour sketched three ways the idea could grow. These are those three, built and shipped as interactive prototypes over the same 25 Northwind files, side by side with the cube they start from. Each is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step; a strip at the top of every one moves between them ([ and ]).

  • 00 · The starting pointShipped first

    FacetOne set of files, three faces

    What it is. A real CSS 3D cube: Project, Client and Type on its faces, two more axes waiting as chips. Select a file and it lights up on every face with connector lines; change one fact in the inspector and it slides to its new group. A Ledger view of the same files and a Compare drawer keep the comparison honest, and a draggable tour explains it.

  • 01 · Time as a fourth axis

    TesseractThe cube, sliced through the quarters

    What it adds over the cube. A time scrubber under the cube slides the whole workspace through 2026 Q1 to Q4; ghost cubes for the neighbouring quarters sit behind it, and world lines trace where a file was and where it goes. A diff panel lists every file that moved, appeared or was archived between two quarters, pin-and-compare holds one quarter beside another, and a timeline under the stage shows each file’s changes as beads on a thread. The faces never change meaning; only the moment does.

  • 02 · One face per axis

    PolyhedronA Platonic solid that morphs as axes come and go

    What it adds over the cube. One face per axis, up to twelve: Status, Owner, Shared with, Size, Modified, Format and Starred join Facet’s five. Switch axes on and off and the solid morphs through the Platonic series, 3 → 4 → 6 → 8 → 12 faces, keeping every file in place. Roll to any axis to bring it square-on, focus a face to read it as a list, pick a preset for the pairs you use most, or unfold the net to see every face flat at once.

  • 03 · Cubes inside cubes

    Nested cubesA group opens into its own child cube

    What it adds over the cube. Depth. Open a group on any face, say Acme on the Client face, and its files become a child cube of their own, grouped by the axes that still tell them apart. The breadcrumb is a path of live mini-cubes you can step back along; an axis override re-groups a child without leaving it, a depth guard stops you drilling past the point where groups hold one file, a mini-map keeps the whole tree in view and sibling compare puts two groups’ cubes side by side.

Built on one foundation

The three new prototypes share one dataset and one toolkit, in prototype/shared/, so the same 25 files behave the same everywhere and a change of grouping means the same thing on a cube, a polyhedron or a child cube.

Every prototype has its own Playwright test under tools/ that drives it end to end, fails on console errors and writes its screenshots.

shared/data.js
The Northwind files with twelve axes and a history per file across 2026 Q1 to Q4, plus filesAt, diff and groupBy helpers.
shared/cube.js
The cube engine from Facet as a factory: faces, drag with inertia and snapping, face enlarge, connector lines, group hover. A page can host one cube or many.
shared/tour.js
The floating, draggable, never-modal tour, generic over any list of steps and any event bus.
shared/chrome.css
The Northwind chrome: tokens, sidebar, top bar, inspector, buttons, chips, toasts, light and dark.
shared/proto-nav.js
The strip above each app that moves between the four prototypes and holds the "About this prototype" note.
